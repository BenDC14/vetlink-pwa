1. Executive Summary & Overview

VetLink is an AI-assisted veterinary clinical decision support and triage Progressive Web Application (PWA) designed to aid licensed veterinary clinicians in identifying canine, feline, avian, and rabbit dermatological lesions. The objective of this review is to evaluate the clinical validity, lexical precision, and regulatory defensibility of the terminology used throughout VetLink's AI screening feature.

Clinical Verdict: The terminology currently implemented in VetLink is remarkably high quality and clinically grounded. The architecture successfully avoids common medical AI flaws by enforcing human-in-the-loop (HITL) oversight and treating AI outputs as tentative clinical triage suggestions rather than autonomous diagnoses. Minor refinements from pure machine learning jargon to formal Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) terminology will further strengthen project defense.

3. Deep Dive: "Confidence Level" in Veterinary CDSS

One of the most important concepts to clarify for your panel is what "Confidence" actually represents in VetLink:

  • What it is in code: The output of the final Softmax layer ($\sigma(z)_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum e^{z_j}}$) of the MobileNetV3 neural network. It indicates the mathematical probability that an input 224×224 RGB image belongs to class $i$ relative only to the other 10 trained classes.
  • What it is NOT: It is not a statistical confidence interval, nor does it guarantee that the patient does not have an unmodeled condition (such as Malassezia dermatitis, flea allergy dermatitis, or cutaneous lymphoma).
Key Defense Talking Point:
"A high neural network confidence score indicates strong visual feature convergence with our validated dataset, but it is never treated as a final diagnosis. In VetLink, the score functions strictly as a triage routing metric: scores ≥ 60% auto-populate a provisional prescription draft for veterinarian review, whereas scores < 60% mandate laboratory confirmation (skin scrapings, tape cytology, or DTM fungal culture)."

Recommended 3-Tier Clinical Score Interpretation

Score Tier Clinical Interpretation System Behavior / Mandate
High Match (≥ 80%) Strong visual concordance with reference pathological presentation. Auto-populates first-line veterinary care protocol into consultation draft; clinician reviews and signs.
Moderate / Ambiguous (60% – 79%) Visual features present, but differential contenders share significant pattern overlap. Generates Differential Advisory banner; prompts clinician to scrutinize lesion borders and consider cytology.
Low Match / Ambiguous (< 60%) Sub-threshold visual certainty; visual mimicry or atypical presentation detected. Mandates formal laboratory diagnostic order (Skin Scrape, Cytology, or Fungal Culture) prior to dispensing.

4. Regulatory & Legal Compliance (Republic Act 9268)

Under the Philippine Veterinary Medicine Act (RA 9268), the diagnosis of animal diseases, prescribing of pharmacotherapeutics, and issuing of official prescriptions are exclusively reserved for registered and licensed veterinarians who have passed the PRC Veterinary Licensure Examination.

VetLink strictly conforms to this legal boundary through three architectural guardrails:

  1. Role-Based Licensure Isolation: Clinic Staff can admit patients and run preliminary AI triage, but cannot generate or alter Rx dispensations.
  2. Mandatory Human Clinical Override: The attending veterinarian retains full authority to override the AI's tentative impression via an integrated dropdown menu.
  3. Verified Credentials & E-Signature: Official prescription pads mandate the veterinarian's PRC License, PTR Number, and verified digital signature before pharmacy release.
